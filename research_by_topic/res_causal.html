Causal Inference

My causal inference research centers on instrumental variables (IV)—methods for estimating causal effects when important confounders are unmeasured—while recognizing that, in real studies, instruments can be imperfect. A main contribution of my work is robust IV methodology: I develop identification and inference tools that remain meaningful even when some instruments may be invalid (e.g., they affect the outcome through pathways other than the treatment), rather than relying on idealized assumptions that all instruments are perfectly valid.
More recently, I have focused on under-identification and non-regular inference that naturally arise in such settings. When identification is fragile, standard confidence intervals—especially the familiar Wald interval—can behave poorly and give a false sense of precision. I develop inference procedures that explicitly account for this instability, providing uncertainty statements that better reflect what the data can (and cannot) justify.
